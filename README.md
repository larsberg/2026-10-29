The scene defaults to seven nearby blobs, each built from five randomly sized, positioned and rotated boxes combined using [Manifold](https://manifoldcad.org/docs/jsapi/documents/Using_Manifold.html) CSG. Each blob has its own reproducible seed derived from the saved herd seed. **Mesh → Box union → Box spread** scales the five box centers within each sculpture from 0× to 4× while preserving seeded sizes and rotations; 1× is the original spacing and 2× is the doubled spacing. Changing spread rebuilds the CSG surface, operation layers, colliders, joints, and skin binding. `src/box-union.js` converts the centered union into shared polygon vertices and triangle faces for the existing mesh operations, surface material and wireframe. Interior overlapping faces are removed by the union.

**Mesh → Herd movement → Number of bodies** sets the herd size from 1–11 in whole bodies, with a default of 7. Changing it rebuilds the seeded formation and saves with the setup; older saves use seven. `src/sculpture-group.js` places the shapes in compact rows of up to three with a small gap based on their finished mesh bounds, starting 0.2 units above the ground. Each sculpture has its own CSG result, operation layers, five-body physics rig, skin, polygon-normal texture, and collision groups: five dynamic colliders per sculpture plus one static ground body. Removed members release their physics, materials, and projection bindings. Blobs can collide and be grabbed independently. Mesh edits, Undo, visibility, material settings, and palettes apply to the entire herd through the existing controls. Camera follow uses their combined bounds, shadow fitting includes every surface, and the initial camera distance frames the group. Reset and regeneration restore the rows.

**Mesh → New variation** changes the saved herd seed and regenerates every set of five boxes. Reload and Reset reproduce the saved seeds and spread. The saved pipeline runs Triangulate → Dialate → Hexelate → Smooth. Smoothing uses four passes at 0.5 strength; each layer remains adjustable in the existing drawer, and manual operations retain Undo. The unions are cached by seed, spread, and body count, so changing operation layers does not repeat the CSG calculation. Resizing updates only the renderer, postprocessing buffers, and camera projection; body poses, manual mesh edits, music position, palette cadence, and animated UV offset continue without restarting.

`src/box-rig.js` binds the processed surface as a Three.js `SkinnedMesh` to five bones. Each dynamic Box3D body uses a convex hull fitted after all mesh operations. `src/collider-fit.js` clips the finished solid to each source box's region, builds its convex hull in the bone's local frame, and samples at most 13 vertices to reduce mobile collision work and stay within Box3D's 255-half-edge limit. This also fits buried boxes that have no exterior vertices assigned to them. Empty regions have no collision shape. Sampling retains the axis extremes and stays inside the full hull; it never restores the old box corners. Hulls remain a rigid approximation of the deforming skin and fill concave recesses.

**Materials → Distance lines** controls one shared 16 × 1 RGBA DataTexture on all bodies and the ground. Each pixel is a seeded random pick from the active **Lighting → Studio palette**, including its lightest color. NearestFilter, RepeatWrapping and no mipmaps keep the repeated bands crisp. **Shuffle line colors** changes the texture seed independently of the body seeds. Each generated body captures every vertex’s original 3D distance to that body’s mass centroid before deformation. This scalar distance supplies the texture U coordinate, scaled by Line spacing and shifted by the existing offset; V stays at 0.5 for the one-row palette. Equal radii sample the same band regardless of direction or body placement. Distances remain fixed through physical deformation and are recaptured only when the mesh is regenerated or edited. Audible harmony chords animate one shared offset for the bodies and ground linearly over 250 ms, reaching the full increment at the end of that interval. The three note events at a chord onset produce one increment, including when they arrive across frames. **Offset speed** runs from −1 to 1 in 0.001 steps (default 0.25), with a 4× multiplier: each chord advances by four times the slider value in palette bands. Negative reverses direction and zero stops increments. The manual offset is additive; the ground and bodies share both manual and musical offsets. Motion pauses with playback and clears on Reset/regeneration. Projection settings and color seed save with the setup. Only seed or palette-color changes upload the texture; offset animation updates a uniform without rebuilding geometry. The existing spotlight and shadows remain unchanged.

The ground samples that same palette texture through a 2D point distance field. Each fitted collider contributes its own current center of mass, projected onto the world XZ plane. Regions with no collision shape are skipped. The shader takes the minimum distance to those points and repeats the texture by that distance, making concentric bands that meet between neighboring sources. **Line spacing** sets radial band width on both the bodies and ground. The old angle and tilt values remain compatible with saved setups, but their controls are hidden because distance mapping has no projection direction. Collider points follow motion and body-count changes, including paused redraws and Reset. Updating the field sends at most 55 2D points (five colliders for each of eleven bodies); it does not regenerate a texture or mesh. The shader compares squared distances and takes one square root per ground fragment. The existing ground material retains its roughness, spotlight penumbra, and shadows. The visible plane and its collision box recenter horizontally under the herd whenever it travels 250 units from the current floor center, keeping the edge out of reach without moving the world-space texture. Planted feet use a separate stationary, shapeless anchor so recentering does not pull the bodies.

The original bone frames and cone constraints are retained. Spherical joints form a tree within each overlapping source cluster, preferring the largest overlaps, with 30° cone/twist limits and damped 2 Hz rotation springs. Only actual source intersections in the generated layout create connections: separated clusters remain unconnected, and isolated boxes have no joints. Changing spread or seed recalculates these connections. Each connected cluster has its own collision group: its intentionally overlapping hulls ignore each other, while detached pieces, other clusters, and outside bodies can collide with it. Collisions between blobs or detached pieces do not create connections. Up to four normalized influences per vertex blend by distance to the oriented source boxes within its nearest cluster; detached skin has no influence from unrelated bones. The GPU skins the surface while matching CPU deformation updates the polygon-normal texture and caches deformed bounds for camera and shadows. Shadow fitting reuses those bounds instead of repeating skinning for every face corner each frame. Wireframe buffers and debug collider poses update only while visible, and refresh immediately when shown while paused. Editing, Undo, Reset, and regeneration refit and rebind the rig without accumulating physics objects.

`src/herd-motion.js` keeps the herd moving with two alternating contact-driven feet per animal. Each has a seeded stride duration and gently changing heading, combining forward travel with a pull toward the group. Low steps, weight shifts, and actual collider contacts produce shuffling and nudges between jumps. Airborne, landing, and grabbed animals release their foot drives; disconnected pieces do not gain artificial joints.

**Harmony chords** trigger one 200 ms flex-and-release pulse per chord, grouping the three note events into a single gesture. Each body uses seeded joint axes. The pulse moves existing spherical-joint spring targets within their original limits, using only free connections in the main body; walking legs, jump preparation, flight, landing, and grabbed bodies take priority. Joint targets and spring settings return to normal afterward. The audible event queue feeds both this response and the shared chord-driven texture offset, and Pause/Reset control them together.

A rear animal prepares each jump over 0.8 beats (0.375 seconds at 128 BPM). Its connected spherical joints curl the lobes inward toward the body center with a smooth increase and brief hold, while its supporting feet crouch. This changes the physical rig and matching skinned surface together. The prepared animal releases its joint targets and foot anchors on the kick. If a grab or loss of ground contact interrupts preparation, that beat rests instead of launching a different unprepared animal. Three landing lanes favor clearance and a continuation of the launch lane, keeping the group compact.

**Mesh → Herd movement → Jump force**, at the top of the Mesh tab, ranges from 0 (no new jumps) to 1 (the previous full strength), and defaults to ⅓. It scales both the launch impulse and added spin. The herd continues shuffling at zero. Edits apply to the next takeoff without rebuilding the herd or changing the beat; Save/Reload retains the value.

**Small jump force (%)**, directly below Jump force, ranges from 0–100 and defaults to 20. On each beat, the other ready, grounded bodies hop toward the current herd center, using this percentage of the current Jump force. The next rear body is reserved for the long jump and excluded from small hops; a short-hop recovery cannot hand its turn to a body farther forward. These small hops add no somersault spin and recover more quickly after landing so the rear bodies can still prepare their larger leaps. Airborne, landing, and grabbed bodies receive no extra launch impulse. Zero disables the small hops; Save/Reload retains the percentage, and older saved setups receive the 20% default.

Takeoff applies one coherent linear/angular impulse across the main connected rig, gated by contact with the ground. The landing target is one jumping-body length beyond the current leading hull edge, measured along the herd heading (including airborne bodies, excluding grabbed ones). The target arc accounts for pack length, body size and gravity; each seeded body adds roughly one forward somersault. The solver handles the entire flight and body collisions, so a collision or lower Jump force can shorten an individual crossing. There is no midair propulsion or transform animation. Low flight damping and continuous collision checks preserve the arc, and a brief high-damping landing phase absorbs the roll before restoring the original physics settings. Reset, grabs and disposal also restore them. Eight solver substeps are used during clenching, fast motion, flight and impact, with four otherwise.

`src/herd-rhythm.js` shares the one-beat cadence with `src/herd-score.js`. At the default 128 BPM, a grounded rear animal launches every 0.46875 seconds on each kick. A shared transport drives audio and movement, including tempo edits, pause, reset, mute and focus suspension. A late frame skips missed launches instead of replaying a burst. If no body can push off, that slot rests rather than launching an airborne body.

Drag a fitted collider with the primary pointer to grab it at the hit point, then release to let it go. A temporary Box3D motor joint pulls that point toward the pointer on a plane facing the camera, allowing the body to rotate and the connected cluster to follow. Picking uses Box3D's native hull raycasts at the current physics poses, so the removed box corners cannot be grabbed. Drag empty space to orbit. Camera follow freezes during a grab. Pointer release/cancel, lost capture, focus loss, Pause, Reset, mesh edits, and disposal release the grab and restore orbit controls. The pointer routing is reused from `../2026-10-16/src/collider-drag.js`.
