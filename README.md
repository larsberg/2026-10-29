The scene starts with seven nearby blobs, each built from five randomly sized, positioned and rotated boxes combined using [Manifold](https://manifoldcad.org/docs/jsapi/documents/Using_Manifold.html) CSG. Each blob has its own reproducible seed derived from the saved herd seed. **Mesh → Box union → Box spread** scales the five box centers within each sculpture from 0× to 4× while preserving seeded sizes and rotations; 1× is the original spacing and 2× is the doubled spacing. Changing spread rebuilds the CSG surface, operation layers, colliders, joints, and skin binding. `src/box-union.js` converts the centered union into shared polygon vertices and triangle faces for the existing mesh operations, surface material and wireframe. Interior overlapping faces are removed by the union.

`src/sculpture-group.js` places the seven different shapes in three compact rows (3/3/1) with a small gap based on their finished mesh bounds, starting 0.2 units above the ground. Each sculpture has its own CSG result, operation layers, five-body physics rig, skin, polygon-normal texture, and collision groups: 35 dynamic bodies plus one static ground body. Blobs can collide and be grabbed independently. Mesh edits, Undo, visibility, material settings, and palettes apply to all seven through the existing controls. Camera follow uses their combined bounds, shadow fitting includes every surface, and the initial camera distance frames the group. Reset and regeneration restore the rows.

**Mesh → New variation** changes the saved herd seed and regenerates all seven sets of five boxes. Reload and Reset reproduce the saved seeds and spread. The saved pipeline runs Triangulate → Dialate → Hexelate → Smooth. Smoothing uses four passes at 0.5 strength; each layer remains adjustable in the existing drawer, and manual operations retain Undo. The seven unions are cached by seed and spread, so resizing or changing operation layers does not repeat the CSG calculation.

`src/box-rig.js` binds the processed surface as a Three.js `SkinnedMesh` to five bones. Each dynamic Box3D body uses a convex hull fitted after all mesh operations. `src/collider-fit.js` clips the finished solid to each source box's region, builds its convex hull in the bone's local frame, and samples at most 13 vertices to reduce mobile collision work and stay within Box3D's 255-half-edge limit. This also fits buried boxes that have no exterior vertices assigned to them. Empty regions have no collision shape. Sampling retains the axis extremes and stays inside the full hull; it never restores the old box corners. Hulls remain a rigid approximation of the deforming skin and fill concave recesses.

The original bone frames and cone constraints are retained. Spherical joints form a tree within each overlapping source cluster, preferring the largest overlaps, with 30° cone/twist limits and damped 2 Hz rotation springs. Only actual source intersections in the generated layout create connections: separated clusters remain unconnected, and isolated boxes have no joints. Changing spread or seed recalculates these connections. Each connected cluster has its own collision group: its intentionally overlapping hulls ignore each other, while detached pieces, other clusters, and outside bodies can collide with it. Collisions between blobs or detached pieces do not create connections. Up to four normalized influences per vertex blend by distance to the oriented source boxes within its nearest cluster; detached skin has no influence from unrelated bones. The GPU skins the surface while matching CPU deformation updates the polygon-normal texture and caches deformed bounds for camera and shadows. Shadow fitting reuses those bounds instead of repeating skinning for every face corner each frame. Wireframe buffers and debug collider poses update only while visible, and refresh immediately when shown while paused. Editing, Undo, Reset, and regeneration refit and rebind the rig without accumulating physics objects.

`src/herd-motion.js` keeps the herd moving with two alternating contact-driven feet per animal. Each has a seeded stride duration and gently changing heading, combining forward travel with a pull toward the group. Low steps, weight shifts, and actual collider contacts produce shuffling and nudges between jumps. Airborne, landing, and grabbed animals release their foot drives; disconnected pieces do not gain artificial joints.

A rear animal prepares each jump over 0.8 beats (0.375 seconds at 128 BPM). Its connected spherical joints curl the lobes inward toward the body center with a smooth increase and brief hold, while its supporting feet crouch. This changes the physical rig and matching skinned surface together. The prepared animal releases its joint targets and foot anchors on the kick. If a grab or loss of ground contact interrupts preparation, that beat rests instead of launching a different unprepared animal. Three landing lanes favor clearance and a continuation of the launch lane, keeping the group compact.

**Mesh → Herd movement → Jump force**, at the top of the Mesh tab, ranges from 0 (no new jumps) to 1 (the previous full strength), and defaults to ⅓. It scales both the launch impulse and added spin. The herd continues shuffling at zero. Edits apply to the next takeoff without rebuilding the herd or changing the beat; Save/Reload retains the value.

Takeoff applies one coherent linear/angular impulse across the main connected rig, gated by contact with the ground. The landing target is one jumping-body length beyond the current leading hull edge, measured along the herd heading (including airborne bodies, excluding grabbed ones). The target arc accounts for pack length, body size and gravity; each seeded body adds roughly one forward somersault. The solver handles the entire flight and body collisions, so a collision or lower Jump force can shorten an individual crossing. There is no midair propulsion or transform animation. Low flight damping and continuous collision checks preserve the arc, and a brief high-damping landing phase absorbs the roll before restoring the original physics settings. Reset, grabs and disposal also restore them. Eight solver substeps are used during clenching, fast motion, flight and impact, with four otherwise.

`src/herd-rhythm.js` shares the one-beat cadence with `src/herd-score.js`. At the default 128 BPM, a grounded rear animal launches every 0.46875 seconds on each kick. A shared transport drives audio and movement, including tempo edits, pause, reset, mute and focus suspension. A late frame skips missed launches instead of replaying a burst. If no body can push off, that slot rests rather than launching an airborne body.

Drag a fitted collider with the primary pointer to grab it at the hit point, then release to let it go. A temporary Box3D motor joint pulls that point toward the pointer on a plane facing the camera, allowing the body to rotate and the connected cluster to follow. Picking uses Box3D's native hull raycasts at the current physics poses, so the removed box corners cannot be grabbed. Drag empty space to orbit. Camera follow freezes during a grab. Pointer release/cancel, lost capture, focus loss, Pause, Reset, mesh edits, and disposal release the grab and restore orbit controls. The pointer routing is reused from `../2026-10-16/src/collider-drag.js`.

**Mesh → Physics → Show colliders** displays green hull-edge outlines through the surface using the same fitted points as physics. It works with surface/wireframe visibility off and changes visibility without resetting the simulation. Spread and collider visibility both use the existing Save/Reload workflow.

The wall plane generator, its controls, and its saved settings are removed. Loading older saves drops the wall fields. The café model remains in `src/cafe-setting.js` for reuse. The custom softbody solver, pin-noise coupling, and Cannon solver are removed. `src/herd-ground.js` supplies the visible shadow-receiving floor and static Box3D collider at Y = 0, using the existing floor color and roughness settings. The camera stays above the ground.

`src/physics.js` initializes `box3d-wasm/standard` 0.2.0, uses fixed 1/120-second steps, and follows play/pause, reset and focus suspension. `window.__meshSketch.physics` exposes the world and managed `addBody({object, body, shapes})` bindings. Shapes accept `type: 'box' | 'sphere' | 'capsule' | 'hull'` plus Box3D options. `window.__meshSketch.mesh.views[i].rig` exposes each sculpture's bones, bodies, joints, weights, and deformed positions; `mesh.rig` routes pointer grabs to the nearest collider across all blobs. The existing gravity and friction controls remain. `boxUnions` exposes all seven seeded sources, `ground` the floor, and `herdMotion.states` the feet and gait phases for inspection.

The loaded Strudel song, **Night romp**, is a 64-bar / two-minute arrangement at 128 BPM: Gather → Romp → Lift → Prowl → Full romp → Turnaround. A deep sine bass repeats one root on all four kick beats, with fundamentals from D1 to A1 (about 37–55 Hz), a low filter, and no octave runs or offbeat fills. Short electric-key chords and a plucked-key question/answer melody provide the playful upper register, moving through D minor 6, F major 7, G minor 6 and A7. The kick also uses the deeper sub voice. Arrangement density, seeded articulation and occasional snare/hat ghosts vary; every kick carries a jump cue, including the lighter alternating accents and the middle section. The bass and kick envelopes also drive step rate, stride length, lift and free-leg rocking through the physical joints; a common transport keeps sound and motion together even when muted. These envelopes come directly from the score without extra FFT work. Query windows and cache eviction never reroll notes. Audio starts when the browser permits playback or the scene is touched; the existing Audio controls set tempo, mute, volume and instrument tuning. Harmony and Lead / texture control the chord and plucked-key layers independently. Recording and `window.__meshSketch.audio.setPattern(pattern)` remain available.
